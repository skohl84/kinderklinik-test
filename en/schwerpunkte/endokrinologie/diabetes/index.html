    Diabetes in children and adolescents

    Diabetes is the most common metabolic disease in children and adolescents in Germany. According to the 2025 German diabetes health report, around 37,000 children and young people live with type 1 diabetes, and about 4,000 under-18s are newly diagnosed every year.

    Recognising warning signs

    Frequent urination, weight loss and great thirst are typical signs. About one in five children is only diagnosed when they already have life-threatening ketoacidosis. Recognising the signs early can prevent this.

    Type 1 diabetes

    In type 1 diabetes the immune system destroys the insulin-producing cells of the pancreas. Children react more strongly to blood sugar fluctuations than adults, and insulin needs change with growth, hormones and infections. Good treatment and blood sugar control reduce long-term complications.

    Type 2 diabetes

    Children and adolescents can also develop type 2 diabetes, and new cases have risen markedly over ten years. The main risk factors are family history, lack of exercise and overweight. A healthy lifestyle can improve the course.

    What we offer

    • Regular, age-appropriate education for children, adolescents and parents
    • All common treatment options, long-term outpatient care
    • Awareness days, for example on World Diabetes Day
    • Sports courses with peers
    • Psychosocial service within the diabetes team, inpatient and outpatient via the SPZ
    • Training for carers in nurseries and schools within the NRW state programme
    • Free outpatient aftercare with Bunter Kreis in the Aachen region, for example during puberty
