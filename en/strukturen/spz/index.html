    Social Paediatric Centre (SPZ)

    The Social Paediatric Centre is an outpatient service of the department. We care for children and adolescents from birth until their 18th birthday with developmental or behavioural disorders, disabilities or a risk of disability.

    Who we care for

    • Severe physical disabilities, e.g. epilepsy, spasticity, neuromuscular disorders, severe visual or hearing impairment
    • Genetic or syndromic conditions, e.g. trisomy, Prader-Willi or Angelman syndrome, neurofibromatosis
    • Autism spectrum disorders
    • Former preterm infants
    • Learning difficulties where a special school is being considered or already attended
    • Infants and preschool children with severe language problems, marked motor limitations, global developmental delay, behavioural or attention problems

    Please do not refer to the SPZ for: isolated reading, spelling or arithmetic disorders, psychiatric problems such as anxiety, depression, compulsions, addiction, eating disorders or tics, or AD(H)D from school year 3 onwards.

    Specialist clinics

    • Neuro-orthopaedic clinic with aids and appliances, with the Department of Orthopaedics
    • Neurogenetic clinic, with the Institute of Human Genetics
    • Follow-up of preterm infants in the level 1 perinatal centre, with neonatology and the hydrocephalus clinic
    • Neuromuscular clinic at the Neuromuscular Centre
    • Syndrome clinic for children with congenital malformations and syndromes
    • Psychosocial clinic for chronic conditions, e.g. autoimmune diseases, epilepsy, inflammatory bowel disease and rare blood disorders
    • Transition clinic for the move to adult care, with the MZEB
    • Palliative care for life-limiting conditions

    Our team

    Paediatricians and neuropaediatricians, nine psychologists, curative education, three speech therapists, occupational therapy, physiotherapy, two social workers and two diabetes educators.

    How it works

    1. Registration: your paediatrician must agree. Register your child by phone or with the registration form. We will contact you once we have received the documents. Depending on the condition and place of residence, treatment is not always possible.
    2. First visit: a doctor decides which further assessments are needed.
    3. Assessment: usually several appointments with different professionals.
    4. Discussion of findings: together we develop a treatment and therapy plan.
    5. Ongoing support: with regular follow-up if needed.

    We work closely with paediatric practices, the public health office, early intervention and family counselling services, nurseries, schools and therapy practices.

    Privately insured?

    Billing is based on a quarterly flat rate agreed with the Association of Private Health Insurers. There is no itemised billing. Please clarify coverage with your insurer or allowance office in advance.
